# Health-aware meal planning: research and launch requirements

Research date and source access date: **2026-10-03**. Status: product-scoping evidence, not clinical advice or approval to claim medical suitability. No application code changed.

Scope update, 2026-10-04: France and the US are the confirmed primary release markets, for individual adults and households including children. Expand to other European countries afterward based on integration feasibility, retaining country-specific review and evidence requirements. The sources below were researched/accessed on 2026-10-03; this scope update does not imply a new source review. Europe is not synonymous with the EU: a UK or other non-EU launch requires its own reviewed guidance and legal assessment.

## 1. Define supported modules rather than promise every condition

Maintain a coverage matrix by **country, age/life stage, condition, required evidence, combined restrictions, reviewer and rule version**. Public-health guidance can inform general variety and balance; it cannot establish an individual's treatment plan. French adult and child guidance already distinguish life stages. [Santé publique France: adults](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/a-tout-age-et-a-chaque-etape-de-la-vie/les-recommandations-alimentaires-pour-les-adultes), [children aged 4–17](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/a-tout-age-et-a-chaque-etape-de-la-vie/enfants-et-adolescents-de-4-a-17-ans/les-recommandations-alimentaires-pour-les-4-17-ans), [USDA life stages](https://www.myplate.gov/web/web/life-stages).

Product requirement: explicitly distinguish **supported**, **requires additional information**, and **not assessed**. A user declining health questions has an unknown profile. An arbitrary condition entered in free text must not silently become a supported module. Named qualified clinical reviewers must validate each module before health-suitability claims or automated recommendations based on it.

## 2. Allergies, intolerances and coeliac disease

Keep these as separate profile concepts, distinct from dislikes. Coeliac disease is autoimmune; it must not be treated as a casual gluten preference or equated with wheat allergy. Do not diagnose from symptoms or infer tolerance from previous consumption. [Assurance Maladie: coeliac disease and distinctions](https://www.ameli.fr/assure/sante/themes/intolerance-gluten-maladie-coeliaque/definition-causes-facteurs-favorisants).

The EU declares 14 allergen groups; FDA rules identify nine major US allergens. Their scopes differ: for example, US wheat is not the EU gluten-containing-cereals group. Neither list exhausts all possible allergies. Support specific foods/species and user-declared additional allergens, with review of matching rules. [European Commission allergen declarations](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/campaign-2026/allergies_en), [FDA allergens and labeling scope](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies).

Proposed behavior: expand compound ingredients, aliases, sauces, stocks, garnishes and substitutions; retain source labels and unresolved ambiguities. “Pesto” without composition is not cleared by ingredient-name matching. Separate **known conflict**, **information unresolved**, and **no conflict identified in checked data**; never equate the latter with “allergy-safe.” A missing precautionary label is not proof that cross-contact is absent. FDA explains manufacturing cross-contact and the limitations of its labeling scope. Brand/variant and current packaging evidence matter; a barcode or generic recipe alone cannot prove suitability. Recheck substitutions and product changes through the same rules.

## 3. Pregnancy and diabetes require distinct evidence

Pregnancy rules depend on preparation, pasteurization, species, storage and local guidance, not merely food categories. French guidance distinguishes cooked versus raw/smoked fish and specific species; FDA resources cover safe selection, handling and preparation. Obtain only relevant profile details and do not invent supplement or energy prescriptions. [Santé publique France: pregnancy](https://www.mangerbouger.fr/manger-mieux/a-tout-age-et-a-chaque-etape-de-la-vie/les-recommandations-et-conseils-avant-pendant-et-apres-la-grossesse/l-alimentation-de-la-femme-enceinte), [FDA pregnancy food safety](https://www.fda.gov/food/people-risk-foodborne-illness/food-safety-pregnant-women-and-their-unborn-babies).

Separate **type 1, type 2, gestational diabetes, and diabetes predating pregnancy**. Clinician-set meal constraints can be recorded with units, applicability, provenance and review date; the app must not invent them. CDC explicitly recommends working with a clinician or dietitian to set carbohydrate goals. HAS type 1 and type 2 resources have different scopes; an older reimbursement-oriented type 1 guide is not sufficient current evidence for a meal-planning algorithm. Gestational support requires current dedicated obstetric/diabetes review. [CDC meal planning](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/healthy-eating/diabetes-meal-planning.html), [HAS adult type 1 guide](https://www.has-sante.fr/jcms/c_419399/fr/ald-n-8-diabete-de-type-1-chez-l-adulte), [HAS type 2 care pathway, June 2025](https://www.has-sante.fr/jcms/p_3636176/fr/parcours-de-soins-du-patient-adulte-vivant-avec-un-diabete-de-type-2-guide), [HAS pregnancy/diabetes care coordination](https://www.has-sante.fr/jcms/c_935540/en/grossesses-a-risque-orientation-des-femmes-enceintes-entre-les-maternites-en-vue-de-l-accouchement).

Product boundaries: no insulin dosing, glucose prediction, medication changes, emergency management or automatic “compensation” after a flexible day or workout. Exercise context can alter practical ranking; clinical implications belong to reviewed care constraints.

## 4. Children and household composition

Plan for the **actual diners in each meal**, with member-specific restrictions, portions and preparation requirements. Do not scale an adult diet mechanically for a child or give adult weight-loss targets to minors. Guardians should manage child profiles; separate adult household permissions must prevent unnecessary access to private health information.

Pediatric scope must explicitly cover supported age bands, growth-oriented nutrition, choking/texture requirements and higher-risk food handling. Infants cannot be assumed supported by an older-child module: CDC describes developmental readiness and texture requirements for starting solids. [CDC introducing foods](https://cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/when-what-and-how-to-introduce-solid-foods.html), [CDC appropriate foods/textures](https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/foods-and-drinks-to-encourage.html), [FDA pregnancy and under-five safety resource](https://www.fda.gov/media/83740/download).

Household members may need separate suitable variants. If no meal satisfies all restrictions, explain the conflict and offer separately checked alternatives; never relax a restriction to fill the week.

## 5. Nutrition estimates and pantry evidence

Ciqual contains mean composition values per 100 g edible food, confidence information and missing values that must not become zeros. USDA FoodData Central distinguishes analytical, survey and branded-label datasets and documents variability. Store dataset/version, food match, raw/cooked state, edible amount, recipe yield, portion allocation and unresolved ingredients. Display estimates with completeness; “fish and salad” cannot establish exact nutrients or deficiencies. [Ciqual 2025 methodology](https://ciqual.anses.fr/cms/sites/default/files/inline-files/Table%20Ciqual%202025%20doc%20FR_2025_11_19.pdf), [USDA dataset types](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/data-documentation/), [USDA variability](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/Foundation_Foods_Documentation/).

Rich pantry metadata can support checks: brand/variant, label image, composition, allergen statements, lot, quantity/unit, storage location, date type, opening/freezing/thawing/preparation dates and confirmation time. Lots support recall matching when a verified recall source exists; dates support prompts, not guarantees. Preserve national date semantics: French DLC/DDM and US dating phrases are not interchangeable. Storage history can invalidate assumptions despite an in-date label. [DGCCRF date guidance](https://www.economie.gouv.fr/dgccrf/les-fiches-pratiques/date-limite-de-consommation-et-date-de-durabilite-minimale-ce-que-vous-devez-savoir), [USDA product dating](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/food-product-dating).

Product requirement: unknown quantities remain unknown; planning reserves stock, cooking records consumption, and leftovers retain their own history. Expiry-priority ranking must never override health restrictions. Software cannot observe cold-chain failures, cross-contact or actual cooking temperatures, so a stocked item is not automatically safe to recommend.

## 6. Privacy, review and release gates

Collect health information when personalization is enabled, with a separate clear purpose and appropriate consent/legal basis; do not bundle health consent with signup terms. Minimize fields, retention and AI disclosures; keep profiles/history out of social posts, logs and general analytics. Provide correction, export, deletion and withdrawal controls. CNIL explains explicit consent for wellness health collection and distinguishes OS permissions from lawful processing. [CNIL health apps](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/applications-mobiles-en-sante-et-protection-des-donnees-personnelles-les-questions-se-poser), [CNIL mobile permissions/minimization](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/permissions-applications-mobiles-recommandations-de-la-cnil-pour-respecter-la-vie-privee).

Child accounts require a distinct legal assessment. EU consent ages depend on country and context; COPPA concerns covered collection from children under 13. Parent-entered household data is not blanket proof of compliance. Review US state consumer-health laws and all applicable jurisdictions before launch. [CNIL consent rules](https://www.cnil.fr/les-bases-legales/consentement), [FTC COPPA assessment and parental consent](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business).

Separate sourced restriction checks from generative explanations. Version rules, record reviewers and checked inputs, re-evaluate affected plans after relevant changes, and test combined restrictions, incomplete recipes, unit ambiguity, children and substitutions. Establish incident reporting, rollback and source-refresh ownership.

Obtain regulatory review of intended purpose, actual behavior and marketing: disease-specific planning can raise medical-software questions; calling it wellness is insufficient. The applicable EU guidance and January 2026 FDA general-wellness policy are assessment starting points, not automatic exemptions. [MDCG 2019-11 rev.1, June 2025](https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/update-mdcg-2019-11-rev1-qualification-and-classification-software-regulation-eu-2017745-and-2025-06-17_en), [FDA general wellness, January 2026](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/general-wellness-policy-low-risk-devices).

Launch gates: defined country/age matrix; qualified clinical, food-safety and privacy review; validated rule/data coverage; uncertainty UX; permission and consistency verification; documented claims and incident process. Completeness means reliable handling of both known and unknown cases, not universal medical coverage.
